School being hard isn't the issue. The issue is pressure that keeps climbing long after it's stopped doing any good.

Two things look identical from the outside. One sharpens you. The other slowly takes you apart.

And the hard part is that it hides. Marks often hold steady for months while the pressure builds underneath — which is why the students everyone worries about last are frequently the ones struggling most.

Recovery

Uplift, in one sentence.

Trained senior students run short weekly sessions for younger years that pair one practical study strategy with one honest conversation — and stay in touch afterwards.

Two findings point the same way. Self-regulated learning — planning, prioritising, breaking work down — measurably cuts academic stress. And near-peers reach students formal services never do: in one controlled trial, trained student leaders got friends to a trusted adult four times more often than untrained students.

Almost nowhere are those two things delivered together. Uplift puts them in the same room, led by someone two years older who says out loud that they found it hard too.

    Four sources. One pressure. One loop.

    • You — perfectionism, fear of failing
    • School — three things due the same week
    • Home — success defined as one number
    • Mates — everyone looks like they're fine
    • Pressure → doing worse → more fear → more pressure

    Who it hits. Who can fix it.

    • Affected — students, especially the high achievers
    • Enabling — wellbeing staff, student leadership, the Principal
    • Both — teachers and parents. They feel it and they set it.

    Uplift.

    Trained senior students run six weeks of short sessions for younger years — one practical strategy, one honest conversation — and stay in touch after.

    • Wellbeing staff train 12 senior students
    • Assembly launch: real stories, told honestly
    • Six weeks, groups of 8–10, 30 minutes
    • Each cohort trains the next one

    And we'll prove it.

    • ESSA stress score, week 1 vs week 6
    • Distress–Eustress Scale — Australian, validated
    • Attendance, help-seeking, assessment clashes
    • These are targets, not results. If they don't move, we change the sessions.
