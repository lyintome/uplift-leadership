How to use these
  • The boxed line is the only thing written out word for word — it's your opener, so you never stall at the start. Everything else is a prompt, not a script.
  • The rubric explicitly penalises reading from cards. Know your beats, then look up and talk.
  • Driver: whoever isn't speaking runs the keyboard. Right arrow = next slide. T starts the timer on slide 1.
  • The clock time is where the timer should be when you finish your card.

Two kinds of stress

  • "Not all stress is bad. There's a version that actually helps — it's called eustress."
  • Sharpens focus, builds confidence, ends when the task ends.
  • "The other kind never switches off. Same face from the outside. Completely different thing."
  • Killer line: "and it hides — marks can look fine for months while this builds underneath."

Walk the model

"So how much pressure is too much? We built a model to find out."
  • Left to right = how much work. Up = how well they're actually doing.
  • "A bit of work, they rise to it — standing taller."
  • "This is their peak. Every student has one."
  • Then the drop: "working harder, doing worse." Let that sit for a second.

The evidence

  • 39% — stress hurting their mental health. Mission Australia Youth Survey, 2025.
  • 49% — in high or very high psychological distress. headspace, last October.
  • Only 1 in 5 who needed help actually went to a counsellor.
  • "So the help exists. Students just aren't using it."

It's a system, not a list

"Here's why this is hard to fix — it doesn't come from one place."
  • You — perfectionism, fear of failing.
  • School — three things due the same week.
  • Home — success defined as one number.
  • Mates — everyone posts the mark, nobody posts the struggle.
  • Then the loop: "pressure makes you do worse, doing worse makes you more scared, being scared adds pressure."

Speaker 4 · Card 1finish by 4:22

What Uplift actually is

"Trained senior students, running six weeks of short sessions for younger years."
  • Each session: one practical strategy + one honest conversation.
  • Why it works: study skills cut stress, and near-peers get through where adults don't — trained student leaders got friends to an adult four times more often.
  • "Right now those two things happen in different rooms. We put them in the same one."
  • Launch is an assembly where seniors tell the truth about their own stress.
  • Each group trains the next one — so it survives us leaving.

And we'll prove it

  • Same validated survey week 1 and week 6 — the ESSA stress scale.
  • Plus the Distress–Eustress Scale, which is Australian and separates good pressure from harm.
  • Attendance, help-seeking, and how many weeks have three-plus tasks due.
  • "These are targets, not results. Uplift hasn't run yet. If the numbers don't move, we change the sessions."
